## How it works

A flex container lines its children up along one axis, called the **main axis**, which `FlexDirection` picks. A `Row`
runs left to right and a `Column` runs top to bottom, and the other axis is the **cross axis**. `JustifyContent` spreads
the children along the main axis when there's room to spare, and `AlignItems` places them across it. `Gap` keeps space
between neighbours, and `Padding` keeps all of them away from the container's edges. When a row runs out of room,
`FlexWrap` lets it continue on a new line instead of overflowing.

Try this sandbox, where five blocks of different sizes sit in a box and each control changes one of its properties:

A container is a row unless told otherwise, like on the web, but items don't shrink unless told to, like in React
Native. `AlignItems` only has work to do when there's free space across the axis, and `JustifyContent` only when there's

## Units

A size, padding, margin, gap or position is a `YogaValue`. `12.pt` is twelve points, which are Unity units, and a plain
`12f` means the same. `50.pct` is half of the parent, and `YogaValue.Auto` sizes from the content and is the default.
Yoga's other keywords are the `YogaValue.FitContent`, `YogaValue.MaxContent` and `YogaValue.Stretch` constants.

A `YogaVector` holds one value per axis, so `new() { x = 20.pt }` sets the width and leaves the height to the content.
A single value like `Size = 7.pt` sets both axes at once. A `YogaFrame` holds one value per edge, like
`new() { left = 2.pt, right = 2.pt }`, and a single value fills all four edges.

In this demo, a slider sets the same number for two bars, in points for one and in percent of the box for the other:

## Growing and shrinking

`FlexGrow` is an item's share of the leftover room, and `FlexShrink` is its share of the shortfall when there isn't
enough. Most of the time you'll use `Flex = 1`, which means grow 1 starting from nothing, so items take equal shares.
Unlike CSS it doesn't also make the item shrink, because Reactive uses Yoga's defaults rather than the web's. Two
siblings with `Flex = 1` split a row in half, and `Flex = 1` next to `Flex = 2` splits it one to two. That's how a label
fills the room next to a button, or a page fills the room next to a sidebar. `MinSize` and `MaxSize` clamp whatever the
rest decided, which also keeps a growing item from being squeezed to nothing.

Below you can see two growing blocks sharing a row with a fixed one, each with a slider for its `FlexGrow`:

As you move the sliders, the fixed block keeps its width while the other two split whatever is left. A few more properties shape a
single item. `Margin` pushes its neighbours and the container's edges away from it, and `AlignSelf` overrides the
container's `AlignItems` for it alone. `AspectRatio` works out one axis from the other, so a box with only a height and
an aspect ratio of 1 is square.

## Absolute positioning

`PositionType.Absolute` takes an item out of the flow, so its siblings are laid out as if it wasn't there. `Position`
then pins it by its distance from the parent's edges, in points or percents, and only the edges you set count. A badge
in a corner, a settings button the rest of the screen shouldn't know about and anything that follows the pointer all work
this way. So does a progress fill behind a label, stretched with `{ left = 0.pt, top = 0.pt, bottom = 0.pt }` and a
bound width.

Here's a badge you can move between the corners of a box without the two blocks inside it noticing:

## Constraining a root

A root is a layout that nobody else lays out, like the top of a screen, so it's sized against the rect of the transform
it's attached to. `ConstrainHorizontal` and `ConstrainVertical` on its controller decide how, one axis each, and both are
on by default. On a constrained axis the root fills that rect, and on an unconstrained one it's as big as its content. A
fixed `Size` on the root still wins over both, and a `MaxSize` makes it fit its content up to that limit. The flags come
from Reactive rather than Yoga, and mean nothing on a flex layout nested in another one, where the parent decides the
size. [Layout System](/docs/reactive/layout/system#leaves-and-roots) explains what makes a layout a root.

Turn either flag off below to see the root, the blue box, shrink to its label on that axis:
